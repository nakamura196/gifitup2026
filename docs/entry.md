## 1. 国芳 — The Face That Falls Apart

- Upload: `yose/final.gif`
- Link to the item: https://jpsearch.go.jp/item/tfam_art_db-9801
- Categories: なし

Anything else (title, description):

> **Title:** He looks fierce, but he is really nice
>
> Utagawa Kuniyoshi's print (c. 1847) is a face made entirely of people. The face yawns, and its jaw, a sleeping man curled into a C, slides out and lands in the face's own hand. The hand turns out to be two more men: they catch him, one sits up for a look, and together they bounce him like a springboard, and he somersaults back into place. The title on the print says "he looks fierce, but he is really a nice person."
>
> Source: Utagawa Kuniyoshi, "Mikake wa kowai ga tonda ii hito da", Tokyo Fuji Art Museum, CC0, via Japan Search.
> Where a figure was hidden behind another in the print, I closed its outline in the print's own ink colour and filled it with that figure's own colours (head, back, arm and hips of the falling man; the back of the lower hand man; the nose man's red loincloth, made by stretching the folds of the print's own red cloth). The falling man is drawn from behind, so no face was added.


## 2. 千蟲譜の蝉 — Science

- Upload: `mushi/final.gif`
- Link to the item: https://jpsearch.go.jp/item/dignl-2576062
- Categories: **I am entering the special category: SCIENCE**

Anything else (title, description):

> **Title:** The page that molts
>
> In Kurimoto Tanshū's insect album Senchūfu (Edo period, 19th century), a painted cicada crouches and flies off across the page. Then the drawn nymph beside it does exactly what the note on the page says: "it climbs out of the ground, grips a branch, its back splits and it slips out of its skin." A soft, pale cicada pushes out of the split, hangs back, pulls itself free, and walks back into place as its wings open and its colour returns.
>
> Source: Kurimoto Tanshū, Senchūfu, National Diet Library, Japan, Public Domain, via Japan Search.
> The only line not in the original is the thin crack on the nymph's back, drawn in the album's own ink colour. The pale cicada is the same drawing, lightened.

## 3. 新訂萬國全圖 — The Flat World Rolls Up

- Upload: `globe/final.gif`
- Link to the item: https://jpsearch.go.jp/item/dignl-2543045
- Categories: なし

> **Title:** Where on Earth is Japan?
>
> A Japanese world map from 1852 shows the round Earth as two flat circles. Here the two hemispheres peel off the paper and close into a globe, spin, and stop on Japan. The map's own red seal jumps from the title and stamps Japan, and the globe unrolls back into the flat map.
>
> Source: Shintei bankoku zenzu (cover title Kaisei bankoku yochi zenzu), 1852, National Diet Library, Japan, Public Domain, via Japan Search.
> The globe is the map's own pixels wrapped onto a sphere with ordinary image warping; the close-up uses a higher-resolution IIIF image of the same item.
